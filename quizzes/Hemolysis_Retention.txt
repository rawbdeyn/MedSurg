QUESTION: What is hemolysis?
A: Premature destruction of red blood cells
B: Increased production of white blood cells
C: Decreased production of platelets
D: Excess production of plasma proteins
ANSWER: A
RATIONALE: Hemolysis is the premature destruction of red blood cells before their normal lifespan of about 120 days. Increased white blood cell production, decreased platelet production, and excess plasma protein production are not definitions of hemolysis.
END

[MCQ]
QUESTION: Approximately how long is the normal lifespan of a red blood cell?
A: 30 days
B: 60 days
C: 90 days
D: 120 days
ANSWER: D
RATIONALE: Normal red blood cells circulate for approximately 120 days before being removed. Lifespans of 30, 60, or 90 days are shorter than the normal RBC lifespan.
END

A: It reduces RBC protection against oxidative stress
B: It increases RBC protection against all oxidants
C: It causes excessive platelet production
D: It prevents hemoglobin oxidation
ANSWER: A
RATIONALE: G6PD deficiency reduces NADPH production and weakens reduced-glutathione-dependent antioxidant defense, making RBCs vulnerable to oxidative stress. It does not increase antioxidant protection or prevent hemoglobin oxidation.
END

QUESTION: Which statement best describes extravascular hemolysis?
A: Macrophages remove RBCs mainly in the spleen and liver
B: RBCs are destroyed only inside arteries
C: Platelets digest RBCs in the bloodstream
D: RBCs are converted into white blood cells
ANSWER: A
RATIONALE: Extravascular hemolysis primarily occurs when macrophages in the spleen and liver remove damaged RBCs. RBCs are not destroyed only inside arteries, platelets do not perform this role, and RBCs do not convert into white blood cells.
END

QUESTION: Which statement best describes intravascular hemolysis?
A: RBCs are destroyed directly within blood vessels
B: RBCs are removed only by splenic macrophages
C: RBCs are stored permanently in bone marrow
D: RBCs are converted into platelets
ANSWER: A
RATIONALE: Intravascular hemolysis is the direct destruction of RBCs within the bloodstream. Splenic macrophage removal describes extravascular hemolysis, while RBC storage and conversion into platelets do not occur.
END

QUESTION: Which finding would support a compensatory bone marrow response to hemolysis?
A: Reticulocytosis
B: Complete absence of reticulocytes
C: Persistent platelet destruction
D: Decreased erythropoietin response
ANSWER: A
RATIONALE: Reticulocytosis indicates that the bone marrow is increasing RBC production in response to RBC loss. A complete absence of reticulocytes and decreased erythropoietin would not represent an effective compensatory response.
END

QUESTION: Which substance increases when heme from destroyed RBCs is broken down?
A: Unconjugated bilirubin
B: Insulin
C: Albumin
D: Fibrinogen
ANSWER: A

ANSWER: A

A: Transfusions and iron chelation
B: Iron supplements and no monitoring
C: Fluid restriction and antacids
D: Antibiotics as the only long-term treatment
ANSWER: A
RATIONALE: Severe transfusion-dependent thalassemia may require chronic transfusions along with iron chelation to manage anemia while preventing transfusional iron overload. QUESTION: Which complication is specifically associated with pulmonary involvement in sickle cell disease?
A: Acute chest syndrome
B: Avascular necrosis
C: Priapism
D: Stroke
ANSWER: A
RATIONALE: Acute chest syndrome is the major acute pulmonary complication of sickle cell disease and involves a new pulmonary infiltrate with compatible symptoms. Avascular necrosis affects bone, priapism affects the penis, and stroke affects the brain.
END

QUESTION: Which complication is specifically associated with cerebral vaso-occlusion?
A: Stroke
B: Acute chest syndrome
C: Pigment gallstones
D: Priapism
ANSWER: A
RATIONALE: Cerebral vaso-occlusion can reduce blood flow to the brain and cause ischemic stroke. Acute chest syndrome affects the lungs, pigment gallstones result from chronic hemolysis, and priapism involves penile blood flow.
END

QUESTION: Which complication is specifically associated with bone ischemia?
A: Avascular necrosis
B: Acute chest syndrome
C: Functional asplenia
D: Hemoglobinuria
ANSWER: A
RATIONALE: Avascular necrosis results from impaired blood supply and ischemia of bone tissue. Acute chest syndrome affects the lungs, functional asplenia results from repeated splenic infarction, and hemoglobinuria reflects intravascular hemolysis.
END

[MCQ]
QUESTION: Which complication is specifically associated with prolonged penile vaso-occlusion?
A: Priapism
B: Stroke
C: Acute chest syndrome
D: Hepatosplenomegaly
ANSWER: A
RATIONALE: Priapism is a prolonged, often painful erection caused by vaso-occlusion of penile blood flow. Stroke affects the brain, acute chest syndrome affects the lungs, and hepatosplenomegaly involves enlargement of the liver and spleen.
END

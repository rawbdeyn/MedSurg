QUESTION: A patient is admitted to the emergency department following a severe motor vehicle collision with a suspected basilar skull fracture. Upon assessment, the nurse notes clear, watery fluid draining from the patient's ears and nose, along with periorbital ecchymosis ("raccoon eyes"). Which nursing intervention is contraindicated for this patient?
A: Elevating the head of the bed 30 to 45 degrees
B: Instructing the patient to gently blow their nose to clear the nasal passages
C: Administering prescribed stool softeners to prevent straining
D: Monitoring closely for signs of meningitis and central nervous system infection
ANSWER: B
RATIONALE: Instructing a patient with a basilar skull fracture and CSF leakage to blow their nose is strictly contraindicated because it increases intracranial pressure and can force pathogens through the cribriform plate, leading to severe intracranial infection. Elevating the head of the bed reduces ICP and CSF leakage, stool softeners prevent Valsalva straining, and monitoring for meningitis is a crucial priority.
END

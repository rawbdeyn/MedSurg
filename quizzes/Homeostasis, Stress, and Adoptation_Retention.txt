A: Increasing local blood flow, leading to redness and warmth at the injury site
B: Decreasing vascular permeability to prevent swelling
C: Triggering systemic cortisol secretion from the adrenal cortex
D: Accelerating the breakdown of nuclear DNA in labile cells
ANSWER: A
RATIONALE: Local vasodilation increases blood flow to the injured area, producing the cardinal local signs of redness and warmth. Capillary permeability increases (not decreases) to allow fluid leakage, and vasodilation is a local vascular event rather than an endocrine or nuclear DNA destruction pathway.
END

[MCQ]
QUESTION: Which cellular regenerative category includes cells that divide continuously throughout life?
A: Permanent cells
B: Stable cells
C: Labile cells
D: Dysplastic cells
ANSWER: C
RATIONALE: Labile cells continuously proliferate and divide throughout life to replace worn-out or damaged tissue (e.g., skin, GI tract). Stable cells divide when stimulated, and permanent cells have minimal or no division capacity.
END
